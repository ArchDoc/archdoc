# A change to design: plan it with the user

Part of the archdoc skill ([SKILL.md](../SKILL.md)), for designing a change with the user before it's built. Make model changes the way SKILL.md's "Change the model by ID" says.


When the user wants to plan, design, or think through a change or a feature before it's built, don't write a plan as a markdown file for them to skim. Plan it with them on the model: they drive the design, saying what they want where they click on the explorer's map (intents, in their own words), and you turn each one into the model against the code, as suggestions they accept or dismiss, asking where they left a choice open, until it's solid. The plan is saved in `.archdoc/plans/<id>.yaml`, detailed enough to carry out.

1. **Start the plan** with the idea as they put it: `archdoc_plan` with `action: start`, `goal`, and `paths` if you know the files (CLI: `archdoc plan start <idea>`). It writes the plan as `drafting`, with the parts of the model the idea touches as its scope.
2. **Open the Plan tab for them**: run `archdoc view --detach --watch --open` yourself (it returns with the URL; don't hand them the command), then point them to Build, then Plan. Tell them to click on the map where they want something: a small menu adds a box there (New element, with what it's for) or a note on what they want (New note); clicking an arrow, or dragging from one box to another, says how two connect. Explore (E) switches clicks to reading the boxes. Each thing they add goes to you as they add it, and your answers show as a conversation beside the map. A box they drew is theirs, drawn as they named it: you work out what it needs (its uses, data, and code) as suggestions beside it. Don't suggest changes or sequences until they've written and sent an intent (respond refuses them), though a note with what you found in the code helps.
3. **Wait for it**: `action: wait` (`archdoc plan wait <id>`). It returns as soon as they add something, with their intents (`i1`, each on the box they clicked) and what they changed since your last read first. `action: read` reads it any time.
4. **Carry out what each intent says, no more**, checked against the code: `archdoc_context` on the box it's on and what the intent names. Answer with `action: respond` and `ops`: the changes (elements, arrows, data) and sequences (flows, as journey steps) it asks for, each with `for: ["i1"]` (respond refuses a suggestion without one) and its own `intent`. Where it leaves a design choice open (which store, sync or queued, a new element or an existing one), don't choose for them: ask with a question on the intent (a note `on: "i1"`, `kind: question`) with `options` (each a few words and what it costs, such as `["Headless Chromium (150 MB)", "Our own SVG renderer"]`), which they tap to answer, and suggest once they answer. An intent with `to` is about how two boxes connect (`on` → `to`, from an arrow they clicked or a drag between boxes): answer with a use, or a new element between them. Add notes pinned to items (`kind: insight` for what you found, `risk` for what could go wrong). Your changes and steps arrive as suggestions; they accept or dismiss them. Never change, accept, or drop their items: suggest beside them, or say why in a note. The plan's check says which intents nothing carries out yet. Tell them what you proposed in a sentence or two; the detail is on the map. It's a conversation, a turn each: answer what's new since your last answer (the unread items), briefly, ask one question at a time, and go straight back to waiting, so they're never waiting on a long review.
5. **Repeat 3 and 4** until nothing's open: every intent carried out, no question unanswered, no suggestion waiting, every item with its intent, and the check clean. Ask before changing a critical journey. They press Mark ready.
6. **Write the tasks** that carry it out (`list: tasks`), each small enough for one commit, with the changes and sequences it carries out (`from`), its files, its tests, and what comes first (`after`). Then carry them out in order, making each change to the model with `archdoc_edit` as its code lands, and set each task's status as you go (`action: task`, `archdoc plan task <id> t1 --status done`): the first sets the plan `executing`, the last sets it `done`. Stop and ask only when `archdoc_check` fails or a task would change a critical journey.

`archdoc_check` checks open plans too, and while one is being carried out, says which changes the model doesn't show yet. A plan is never part of the model.
